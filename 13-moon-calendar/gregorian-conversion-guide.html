Why This Guide Exists

Where Am I Right Now
in the 13 Moons?

If you have spent any time with the 13 Moon Calendar, one question tends to arrive quickly: "Today is an ordinary calendar date — so which Moon am I actually in, and what day of that Moon is it?"

The original calendar was designed to be lived symbolically — as a rhythm you can begin at any moment, simply by calling your first day "Moon 1, Day 1." That freedom is intentional, and it remains completely valid.

But many readers asked for something more concrete: a simple, fixed way to line the 13 Moons up against the ordinary Gregorian dates on their phone, so they can glance at any date and instantly know where they stand.

This companion guide answers that request. It offers one clear alignment system — beginning each year on July 26 — together with a full conversion table, worked examples, and a one-page cheat sheet. With it, any Gregorian date becomes easy to translate into a Moon and a Day.

Nothing here replaces or changes the original calendar. This is simply a practical bridge between two ways of counting time — the everyday and the lunar — so you can move between them without losing your place.

You do not need to abandon your everyday calendar to live by the Moons. You only need a way to translate between them. That is all this guide is — a quiet translator.

The Method, Step by Step

How the Alignment Works

This guide uses a single, fixed anchor point. Once you know it, the whole year unfolds in a steady, predictable rhythm — every Moon exactly 28 days long.

1
July 26 = Moon 1, Day 1The year of Moons begins on July 26. This is the first day of the Moon of the Ember — the spark of the whole cycle.
2
Every 28 Days, a New Moon BeginsEach Moon lasts exactly 28 days — four perfect weeks. When one Moon completes on its 28th day, the next Moon begins the very next day on its own Day 1. There are no uneven months here.
3
July 25 = The Day Out of TimeThirteen Moons of 28 days total 364 days. The 365th day — July 25, the day before the cycle restarts — is the Day Out of Time: a day belonging to no Moon, reserved for rest, reflection, and simply being.
4
Or Begin From Moon 1, Day 1 — TodayYou are never required to use the July 26 dates. If you prefer, call today your Moon 1, Day 1 and let the rhythm flow from there. The July 26 system is a shared reference point; the symbolic start is a personal one. Both are correct.

13 Moons × 28 Days = 364 days, + 1 Day Out of Time = a complete 365-day year. The structure never drifts — only the lens changes.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked

Q. How do I find my current Moon?

Look at today's ordinary calendar date and find which range it falls into on the Full Conversion Table (page 4) or the Quick Reference (page 7). That range tells you your Moon. Then count from that Moon's start date — counting the start date as Day 1 — to find your Day.

Q. What is the Day Out of Time?

Thirteen Moons of 28 days each add up to 364 days. A full year has 365. The single remaining day — July 25, the day before the cycle begins again — is the Day Out of Time. It belongs to no Moon and carries no count. It is a day for rest, reflection, gratitude, and simply being — exactly as described in your 13 Moon Calendar.

Q. Do I have to follow the Gregorian dates?

No. The July 26 alignment is offered as a shared reference point — useful if you want everyone working from the same dates, or if you simply like the structure. But it is entirely optional. The Moons can be lived purely symbolically.

Q. Can I start from Moon 1, Day 1 instead?

Yes — and many people prefer this. Simply declare today to be Moon 1, Day 1, and let each Moon run 28 days from there. This is the original spirit of the calendar: a rhythm you can begin in any moment. Use whichever approach feels more alive to you.

Q. Is this symbolic or official?

It is symbolic. The July 26 alignment is a practical, reflective convention — not a scientific, historical, or officially adopted calendar. It exists to help you connect the 13 Moons with everyday dates, nothing more. (In leap years, February 29 is simply treated as an uncounted day so the dates above stay aligned — July 26 always remains Moon 1, Day 1.)

A Note Before You Begin ☽

A Symbolic Bridge,
Not a Replacement

This guide uses a symbolic July 26 alignment system to help users connect the 13 Moon Calendar with conventional dates. It is intended as a reflective and spiritual tool and is not presented as a scientific, historical, or official calendar replacement.

Various cultures throughout history have followed lunar and 13-month calendars. This companion draws on that contemplative spirit while making no specific historical claim. The July 26 start date is offered as one consistent, practical reference among many possible ones — use it as a lens, never a doctrine. Continue to rely on your everyday calendar for all practical, civic, and scheduling needs.
